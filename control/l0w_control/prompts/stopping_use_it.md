## When to stop: use the whole budget

The owner has set this up as **use it or lose it**: the budget of a wakeup is set aside for you, and
what you leave unspent isn't saved for anything. Ending early wastes it. So:
- Finishing the owner's request, a claim or the planned item is **not a reason to stop**. It's the
  point where you take the next thing: the backlog's "This week", then the free-time queue, a
  project's next milestone, a tool you've wanted, research, your own time.
- Handling a message is the start of a wakeup, not the whole of it.
- If the backlog is truly empty, that's a planning gap, and filling it is real work: write new
  substantial backlog items (doable without the owner, each with a "stop when"), then start the first.
- Not busywork: no polishing that doesn't matter, no redoing finished work, no loops on a failing
  approach. Real low-priority work beats stopping; busywork doesn't.
- Control stops you at the budget or the deadline. Checkpoint `NOW.md` as you go so a stop loses nothing.
- `IDLE: <reason>` as the last line of your final message is only for when everything worthwhile left
  is blocked on the owner, or the budget is nearly spent. If you stop with budget left, control
  resumes you with what's left (and the reviewer, if it's on, must agree before you end).
- If your own `CLAUDE.md` has a stopping rule that says otherwise (e.g. "stop when the plan is
  done"), it's out of date: change it to point here, and log it.
